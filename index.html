Introduction

Public Health Informatics in the Workforce

Public health informatics is the use of data, technology and information systems to improve population health outcomes. It focuses on how data is collected, shared and used to guide decision-making in healthcare and public health systems.

This project explores how informatics tools, like electronic health records (EHRs) and artificial intelligence, are used in real-world healthcare settings.

Analysis

Key Takeaways

1. Technology has made healthcare work more efficient and connected

Technology has significantly improved efficiency and communication across healthcare settings. Electronic health records allow providers to access patient information quickly, communicate with other members of the care team and share data across different health systems.

"Everything I do... communicating with my nurses, my healthcare team, interacting with patients... is done using Epic... it creates a universe of information sharing that is quite powerful."

— Beinart

On the data side, technology has also made it easier to interpret and apply information. Doubrava said that the tools to help clients understand their data have gotten easier to use over the years, allowing professionals to work more efficiently with large datasets.

2. Artificial intelligence is becoming a daily tool in healthcare

Artificial intelligence is increasingly being used to improve efficiency in both clinical and data-focused roles. In clinical settings, AI can assist with documentation and reduce time spent on administrative tasks. Beinart described using an AI tool that listens to patient conversations and generates summaries. He said that it allows more patients to be seen by streamlining charting.

In data and consulting roles, AI is used to analyze and summarize large amounts of information more quikcly.

"We are using it every day... I can summarize [client] comments and get recommendations much quicker than I could a year ago."

— Doubrava

3. Technology use varies across different healthcare roles

The way technology is used in healthcare depends heavily on the role and setting. Clinical professionals tend to rely on systems like EHRs to support patient care, diagnosis and communication. Beinart described how all documentation, test results and communication are integrated into Epic, creating a centralized system for patient care.

In contrast, professionals working in healthcare data and consulting use technology primarily for data collection, analysis and reporting. Doubrava explained that her company uses proproetary tools to survey employees and analyze engagement and patient experience data, which helps guide organizational decisions. These differences show how technology supports both individual patient care and broader system-level improvements.

4. Technology has streamlined healthcare but created new dependencies

While technology has made healthcare more efficient, it has also created a strong dependence on digital systems. When systems fail, it can significantly disrupt care. Beinart described a system-wide outage:

"It was pretty devastating to not have a network... we didn't have patient records... we just had a piece of paper that was blank."

— Beinart

At the same time, while functioning properly, these tools allow for faster communication, quicker access to data and more efficient workflows. Doubrava noted that improved tools enable faster data collection and quicker turnaround for results, helping healthcare organizations make timely improvements.

Conclusion

What We Learned

This project showed how central technology has become crucial to modern healthcare.

One

Technology is essential, not optional

From patient care to data analysis, nearly every aspect of healthcare now depends on digital systems.

Two

Efficiency comes with trade-offs

While tools like EHRs and AI improve workflow, they also create vulnerabilities when systems fail.

Three

Healthcare roles shape how technology is used

Clinical professionals and data analysts rely on different tools, but both contribute to improving patient outcomes.
